## Plain-language summary (read this first)

A few terms, defined once:

- **Workforce IAM**: identity and access management for an organization's own staff/employees — provisioned by HR/admin action, small user count, optimized for least-privilege and audit.
- **CIAM** (Customer Identity and Access Management): identity management for an organization's external customers — self-registered, large/unpredictable user count, optimized for low-friction sign-up and login.
- **Maker-checker** (also "dual control," "4-eyes"): the standard banking control where the person who *initiates* a sensitive action (the maker) cannot also be the person who *approves* it (the checker) — a stricter special case of the more general "segregation of duties."
- **Bounded context** (from Domain-Driven Design): a boundary within which a specific model and vocabulary apply consistently; boundaries are usually drawn where stakeholders, language, or rate of change differ.
- **Backend-for-frontend (BFF)**: a server-side component built specifically to serve one frontend, holding real credentials/sessions server-side so the browser never sees them — already GARBATI's confirmed design for the ops console (D-13).
- **Just-in-time (JIT) / break-glass access**: a temporary, time-boxed elevation of privilege, normally gated by its own approval step and auto-expiring, kept as a distinct, narrowly-scoped mechanism rather than folded into ordinary role design.

**Short answers to the five questions below:**

1. Real systems treat "who is a customer" and "who is our own staff" as architecturally distinct identity domains almost everywhere this was checked — the industry has separate names for them (CIAM vs. workforce IAM) precisely because they optimize for opposite things (friction-free self-service vs. least-privilege/audit). Core-banking vendors (Mambu, Temenos) keep staff/"users" as a structurally separate concept from customers/"clients" inside their own platforms, and commonly delegate staff authentication to an external directory/IdP (Active Directory, Okta, SAML/OIDC SSO) rather than customer-style self-service login.
2. Real back-office consoles have a small, named set of roles — Teller/Cashier, Customer Service Officer, Compliance/KYC Officer, Supervisor/approver, Administrator, Auditor (read-only), Risk/Operations Officer — built around maker-checker: the person who initiates a sensitive action is a different person from the one who approves it. GARBATI's own reversal-authority design (`Garbati.IdentityApi/docs/DESIGN.md` Section 8) already assumes this discipline exists "inside tooling this document does not design and that does not exist yet" — this research confirms that assumption matches standard banking practice, and flags that *this console is that tooling*.
3. Staff authentication for an internal back-office web app typically uses server-side sessions with `HttpOnly`/`Secure`/`SameSite` cookies (not JWTs stored in the browser), issued by exactly the kind of BFF GARBATI has already decided to build — chosen specifically because sessions can be revoked instantly (termination, role change, suspected compromise), which a self-contained JWT cannot do without an extra blocklist. Enterprise-scale back offices often add SSO (SAML/OIDC against a corporate IdP); at GARBATI's current one-institution, small-staff scale, a local staff login inside the BFF (or the staff-identity service) is a reasonable starting point, with SSO as a later upgrade, not a day-one requirement.
4. A realistic role model needs more than "role = fixed permission set": branch/jurisdiction-scoped roles, separation-of-duties conflict rules enforced both at role-assignment time and at action time, and a distinct (not bolted-on) path for temporary/emergency elevated access. At GARBATI's current scale (one institution, presumably one location, a handful of staff), most of this is a deliberate "note for later" rather than something to build now — the one piece worth building from day one is the maker-checker/SoD check itself, because GARBATI's reversal-authority and joint-account designs already depend on it existing somewhere.
5. **Recommendation (input to the owner's decision, not the decision):** lean toward a new, small, dedicated service for staff identity, not a bounded context grafted onto `IdentityApi`'s existing tables — but built the same low-cost way GARBATI already built `ProductApi`/`OrchestratorApi` (a separate project/module with its own database, not necessarily its own deployment on day one). The honest trade-offs for both options are laid out below; this is a real judgment call, not a settled industry answer.

## Q1: How do real banks/core-banking platforms separate customer identity from staff/internal-user identity?

**Short answer:** Separately, almost everywhere checked — described using an industry-standard vocabulary (CIAM vs. workforce IAM) precisely because the two have opposite design priorities, and core-banking vendors keep "staff/user" and "customer/client" as structurally distinct concepts inside their own platforms, commonly with staff authentication delegated to an external corporate directory/SSO rather than built as customer-style self-service login.

- **The industry has two different names for these two things, not one.** "IAM is used to manage employee, contractor, and internal workforce access to business systems, while CIAM manages customer identities, registration, login, consent, profile data, and secure access to customer-facing applications" — and the difference is about scale and design intent, not just terminology: workforce IAM "governs employees, contractors, and internal stakeholders — provisioned from an HR system, numbering in the thousands, optimized for governance, least privilege, and audit," while CIAM "serves external consumers who register themselves, scales to millions... and optimizes for a frictionless experience." [Scalekit: Workforce IAM and Customer IAM](https://www.scalekit.com/blog/workforce-iam-vs-customer-iam); [Curity: CIAM vs IAM](https://curity.io/resources/learn/iam-vs-ciam/). This maps onto GARBATI almost exactly: `IdentityApi` today is a CIAM-shaped system (customers self-register via a channel, KYC-gated, built for the "customer" actor), and the ops-console need is a workforce-IAM-shaped problem (a handful of staff, provisioned by an admin, needing least-privilege and audit).
- **Core-banking vendors keep the two concepts structurally separate inside their own platforms.** In Mambu, "a *user* is anyone who accesses and uses Mambu via the UI or the API" and is assigned a *role* (`administrator`, `teller`, `credit officer` are named standard role types) — a completely different entity from a *client* (Mambu's customer record). [Mambu: Understanding Users, Roles, and Permissions](https://docs.mambu.com/docs/understanding-users-roles-and-permissions/); [Mambu: Roles](https://support.mambu.com/docs/roles). Mambu also supports **federated authentication**: staff/"users" can be provisioned and authenticated through an external Identity Provider (Okta, Google) via SSO, with roles mapped from the IdP rather than Mambu's own local login — "user roles and permissions are managed through the IdP, overriding previous Mambu settings." [Mambu: Managing Users under Federated Authentication](https://docs.mambu.com/docs/managing-sso-provisioned-users/). Temenos's platform similarly defines its own catalogue of internal roles (Administrator, System Manager, Operations, System Support, etc.) as a distinct concept from the customer/applicant/"end user" roles in the same product family. [Temenos Journey Manager: Standard Roles](https://journey.temenos.com/docs/Roles/StandardRoles.htm).
- **Directory-integrated SSO for staff is the standard pattern, independent of the core banking vendor itself.** "Microsoft Active Directory (AD) is the usual implementation; apps talk to it using the LDAP protocol... In many companies, users and passwords live in a central directory, not in each application's database." [DEV Community: Enterprise authentication with LDAP and Active Directory](https://dev.to/blackie360/enterprise-authentication-with-ldap-and-active-directory-2aif). Vendors selling into community banks explicitly build connectors for this: "native connectors for the cores community banks use, including FIS, Jack Henry, and Fiserv, along with directories such as Active Directory" so that staff identity is centrally managed once and every core-banking tool trusts that same directory rather than keeping its own staff login. [Provision: Community Bank IAM](https://provisioniam.com/provision-for-banks).
- **Regulatory guidance treats staff/internal access as its own governed category, independent of customer-facing systems.** FFIEC's Information Security Handbook requires a dedicated "user access program," least-privilege provisioning tied to job role, timely deprovisioning on termination/role change, and "independent monitoring of the activities performed by the users with increased privileges" — guidance written entirely in terms of employees/job roles, with no customer-identity framing at all. [FFIEC IT Examination Handbook — User Security Controls](https://ithandbook.ffiec.gov/it-booklets/information-security/ii-information-security-program-management/iic-risk-mitigation/iic7-user-security-controls).

**What I could not verify:** no source states a single universally "correct" architecture for *where in a microservices decomposition* staff identity must sit (same service as customer identity vs. a separate one) — the CIAM-vs-IAM distinction is well documented as a conceptual/product separation, but none of the vendor or regulatory sources found make a direct claim about service boundaries in a microservices-style core-banking build like GARBATI's. That inference (Q5 below) is this researcher's reasoning from the evidence, not a cited "best practice for service decomposition."

## Q2: What roles does a typical bank back-office console have?

**Short answer:** a small, named, fairly consistent set — Teller/Cashier, Customer Service Officer, Compliance/KYC Officer, Supervisor/approver, Administrator, Auditor (read-only), Risk/Operations Officer — organized around maker-checker: the person who initiates a sensitive action must be a different person from the one who approves it. This matches, and confirms, the assumption GARBATI's reversal-authority design already made.

- **Named roles found in real job descriptions and vendor role catalogues:**
  - **Teller/Cashier**: front-line role that "must verify customer identity, adhere to KYC/AML regulations, file Currency Transaction Reports for transactions exceeding reporting thresholds, and flag suspicious activity to the branch's compliance officer." [Job description sources, aggregated via search](https://jobdescription.org/jobs/finance/bank-supervisor).
  - **Supervisor/Head Teller**: "oversees the teller line, manages vault operations, approves overrides and exceptions, conducts cash audits" and "reviews and approves transactions above teller authority limits" — i.e. the checker for a teller's maker actions. [Work.chron.com: Job Descriptions for Operations Supervisors of Banking](https://work.chron.com/job-descriptions-operations-supervisors-banking-24201.html).
  - **Compliance/KYC Officer**: "ensure staff compliance with BSA/AML requirements including CTR filing, suspicious activity identification, and CIP verification" — a role distinct from both teller and supervisor, focused on regulatory review rather than transaction approval.
  - **Auditor**: "independently performing and/or overseeing test work to evaluate compliance risks... independently designing and executing audit tests" — explicitly independent of the operational chain, matching GARBATI's own task framing of "Auditor (read-only)."
  - **Administrator** and simple functional roles (**Teller**, **Credit/Operations Officer**) are Mambu's own named standard role *types*. [Mambu: Roles](https://support.mambu.com/docs/roles). Temenos's internal catalogue separately names **Administrator**, **System Manager**, **Operations**, **System Support** as its standard internal roles, with Administrator explicitly flagged as "reserved for deployment activities, not production use" — i.e. even the most privileged role is expected to be used sparingly, not as a default working account. [Temenos Journey Manager: Standard Roles](https://journey.temenos.com/docs/Roles/StandardRoles.htm).
- **Maker-checker is the organizing principle behind these roles, not an optional extra.** "A core principle of dual-control authorization systems is that the person who initiates a request cannot approve their own request... maker also serving as checker is prohibited, as one person can't independently validate their own work." [Wikipedia: Maker-checker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maker-checker); corroborated by FFIEC's segregation-of-duties guidance (Q1) and by a dev.to case study on building banking RBAC, which implements explicit "separation rules that prevent incompatible permission combinations — for example, the person initiating a payment cannot authorize it," checked "at role assignment time (preventing conflicting roles) AND at action time." [DEV Community: Building RBAC for Enterprise Systems — Lessons from Banking Software](https://dev.to/tundeoladejo/building-role-based-access-control-for-enterprise-systems-lessons-from-banking-software-20ni).
- **Direct check against GARBATI's existing assumption.** `Garbati.IdentityApi/docs/DESIGN.md` Section 8.1 says a reversal request reaches `IdentityApi` "only from a trusted ops channel... Whatever maker-checker / dual-control discipline that ops channel enforces internally — who may request a reversal, who must approve it, what evidence they review — happens **before** it ever calls `IdentityApi`, inside tooling this document does not design and that does not exist yet," and Section 8.8 repeats: "The ops channel itself... does not exist and is not designed here." **This research confirms that assumption matches standard banking practice exactly** (maker-checker, initiator ≠ approver, is the real-world norm, not a GARBATI invention) — and flags the direct consequence: the ops console being planned now (D-13's console) *is* that tooling. Its role model is where GARBATI's maker-checker discipline for reversals will actually live for the first time; `IdentityApi` itself only ever sees one trusted caller (the ops channel's service identity, via the `identity.ops.reversal` scope) and has no concept of which individual staff member asked for what. `IdentityApi`'s design deliberately pushed that responsibility outward — the console is where it lands.

**What I could not verify:** no single source lists a canonical, complete "bank back-office role taxonomy" as a named industry standard — the roles above are a synthesis across job-description sources, two core-banking vendors' own role catalogues, and general dual-control/SoD guidance, not a single cited "the standard eight roles" document. Treat the specific role list as a reasonable, evidence-backed synthesis, not an industry-ratified standard.

## Q3: How does staff authentication typically work for an internal back-office web app?

**Short answer:** server-side sessions with `HttpOnly`/`Secure`/`SameSite` cookies, issued by a backend component the browser trusts but never sees inside — the same shape GARBATI has already decided to build (a BFF holding real credentials server-side). SSO against a corporate identity provider is the enterprise-scale upgrade; it is not a prerequisite for a small staff count.

- **Sessions, not browser-held JWTs, are the standard recommendation for exactly this kind of app.** "Internal apps prioritize security and simplicity over scalability, with instant revocation (on role change or termination) being critical — something sessions provide natively... Sessions work best for traditional server-rendered apps, internal tools, and admin dashboards where you control the entire stack." The reasoning is specific and directly relevant to a staff console: "you need to revoke sessions immediately — logging a user out of all devices, responding to a compromised account, or enforcing role changes mid-session all require instant revocation, which JWTs cannot do without a blocklist." [OneUpTime: JWT vs Session-Based Authentication](https://oneuptime.com/blog/post/2026-02-20-jwt-vs-session-authentication/view).
- **OWASP's own guidance for browser apps is the same: avoid tokens in the browser, prefer cookies or a BFF.** "OWASP recommends not storing authentication tokens, session IDs, or JWTs in `localStorage` or `sessionStorage`... Instead, use `HttpOnly; Secure; SameSite=Strict` cookies (preferred) or a Backend-for-Frontend pattern." [OWASP Session Management Cheat Sheet](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Session_Management_Cheat_Sheet.html). The standard BFF authentication flow is: the BFF performs the actual login/OAuth exchange as a confidential server-side client, "receives and stores tokens securely in a server-side session, and creates a session cookie marked as `HttpOnly`, `Secure`, and `SameSite=Strict`" for the browser. [Auth0: The Backend for Frontend Pattern](https://auth0.com/blog/the-backend-for-frontend-pattern-bff/). This is directly actionable for GARBATI: the BFF the owner has already decided to build is precisely the component that should hold the staff session, never issuing a bearer token the React app has to store itself.
- **One more concrete, directly relevant point from the same evidence:** "even if the BFF performs OAuth authentication, administrator-function permission is not automatically granted. The BFF confirms the session's user, client, scope, and target resource, and the domain service must also re-verify permissions within its trust boundary" — i.e. the BFF authenticating a staff member is not the same check as authorizing a specific privileged action; whichever service owns staff roles must still be asked, per request, whether *this* staff member may do *this* thing. [Zuplo: Backend for Frontend (BFF) Authentication](https://zuplo.com/blog/backend-for-frontend-authentication).
- **Enterprise SSO (SAML/OIDC against a corporate IdP, LDAP/Active Directory) is the standard upgrade path at larger scale**, not a universal starting requirement. "SSO is best for enterprise environments with multiple related services. For internal tools, session-based authentication is recommended as the primary approach with OAuth/company SSO as a secondary option" ([OneUpTime](https://oneuptime.com/blog/post/2026-02-20-jwt-vs-session-authentication/view)); this matches the vendor pattern already found in Q1 (Mambu's federated-authentication option, AD/LDAP connectors sold specifically to community banks) — real systems treat directory-integrated SSO as something you add once staff count and IT maturity justify the integration cost, not as a day-one must-have for a small shop.
- **MFA for privileged actions** is a recurring theme across general IAM guidance (not specific to any one source above) and matches GARBATI's own existing pattern of treating privileged operations (freeze, reversal) as scopes requiring extra care — worth flagging as a design input for whoever builds this (e.g. require a second factor before a Supervisor approves a reversal), without a specific vendor citation pinning down a concrete mechanism.

**What I could not verify:** no source gives a Nigerian-microfinance-specific or CBN-specific staff-authentication requirement (MFA mandate, session length, etc.) — this is general enterprise/IAM-industry guidance, not a sector-specific regulatory citation. A compliance officer should confirm whether CBN or any applicable Nigerian regulation imposes specific staff-authentication controls before this is built.

## Q4: What does a real role model need that "role = fixed permission set" misses?

**Short answer:** branch/jurisdiction scoping, enforcement of separation-of-duties conflicts at both assignment time and action time, and a distinct (not bolted-on) mechanism for temporary/emergency elevated access. At GARBATI's current scale, most of this is realistically a "note for later" — the one piece worth treating as a day-one requirement is the SoD/maker-checker check itself, because existing designs (reversal authority, joint-account approvals) already assume it exists somewhere.

- **Scope, not just a flat role name.** "Instead of global roles, implement scoped role assignments where 'the same manager role means different things in different departments.' Each user role should include scope metadata (department, project, resource)... tying permissions to organizational context rather than making them globally applicable" — e.g. "Manager of Department X" is a meaningfully different grant from "Manager of Department Y," even though both are nominally "Manager." [DEV Community: Building RBAC for Enterprise Systems — Lessons from Banking Software](https://dev.to/tundeoladejo/building-role-based-access-control-for-enterprise-systems-lessons-from-banking-software-20ni). For a bank, the natural scope axis is **branch or jurisdiction** — a Teller role at Branch A should not, by default, let someone see or act on Branch B's customers. GARBATI's `IdentityApi` already has a `JurisdictionCode` on the *customer* record (`docs/DESIGN.md` 2.1); a future staff-role model scoped the same way would be a natural, low-friction fit, but there is no branch concept for *staff* yet.
- **Separation-of-duties conflicts need an explicit, enforced rule — not an assumed one.** The same source implements "separation rules that prevent incompatible permission combinations... checked at role assignment time (preventing conflicting roles) AND at action time" — i.e. a system should refuse to let someone *be assigned* both "can initiate a reversal" and "can approve a reversal," and should also refuse, at the moment of approval, to let the same person who initiated a specific request also approve that specific request, even if their combination of roles would technically allow it in the abstract. Regulatory guidance backs the same two-layer check: FFIEC calls for both role-based restriction and "independent monitoring of the activities performed by the users with increased privileges" as a second, ongoing layer beyond the initial role grant. [FFIEC IT Examination Handbook — User Security Controls](https://ithandbook.ffiec.gov/it-booklets/information-security/ii-information-security-program-management/iic-risk-mitigation/iic7-user-security-controls).
- **Temporary elevated access should be its own narrow mechanism, not a permanent role upgrade.** "Just-in-Time access methods should be used for elevated privileges required only in sporadic circumstances and for break-glass emergency access procedures, with JIT access interfacing with approval workflows, expiring elevated access after a predetermined period automatically"; and "break glass accounts should be defined as a separate, tightly monitored role outside the normal catalog so emergency access does not get tangled into day-to-day role design." The same evidence also flags a real implementation trap worth remembering if GARBATI ever builds this: "time-boxed and revoked-for-cause access can outlive its window by up to a minute" if expiry relies on a cache instead of being checked live — i.e. a time-boxed grant's expiry must be enforced at the point of use, not just scheduled. [nhimg.org: designing RBAC roles without privilege creep](https://nhimg.org/faq/how-should-security-teams-design-rbac-roles-without-creating-privilege-creep/); [DEV Community](https://dev.to/tundeoladejo/building-role-based-access-control-for-enterprise-systems-lessons-from-banking-software-20ni).
- **Role changes requiring their own approval** is a recurring theme in the same sources (role assignment itself going through a review/approval step, not just granted ad hoc by an administrator) but is not found with a specific, citable banking example as detailed as the SoD or JIT points above — treat this as a reasonable, lower-confidence extension of the same general principle (the act of granting privilege is itself a privileged, auditable action) rather than a separately well-evidenced requirement.
- **What's realistic for GARBATI right now vs. a deliberate "later" note:**
  - **Build now:** a role = permission-set model with an explicit SoD rule for maker-checker-sensitive actions (reversal request vs. approval, at minimum) — this is not optional, because `IdentityApi`'s existing reversal-authority design (Section 8) already assumes this discipline exists and the console is where it must finally be implemented.
  - **Note for later, don't build yet:** branch/jurisdiction scoping (GARBATI is one institution; whether it is even multi-branch yet is unclear from anything read in this research), JIT/break-glass temporary elevation (meaningful once staff count and shift patterns justify it), and a formal role-change-approval workflow (reasonable at a larger staff count; likely overhead for a solo-owner-run shop's first few staff accounts). Flagging these explicitly now, rather than letting them be silently forgotten, is the point of naming them here.

**What I could not verify:** no source gives a specific "minimum viable role model size" for a bank with GARBATI's current scale (one institution, presumably a handful of staff) — the "build now / later" split above is this researcher's judgment applied to the evidence, not a cited recommendation calibrated to a shop this size.

## Q5: Recommendation — bounded context inside `IdentityApi`, or a brand-new dedicated service?

**This is a recommendation for the owner to accept, change, or reject — it does not resolve D-13, and it is not a settled industry answer; no source found states a universal "correct" answer to this specific service-boundary question for a system GARBATI's size.**

### The case for a new, small, dedicated service (this researcher's lean)

1. **Real-world precedent treats "staff/workforce identity" and "customer identity" as different domains with different owners, almost everywhere checked (Q1).** CIAM and workforce IAM are named separately precisely because they optimize for opposite things — self-service/low-friction vs. least-privilege/audit — and core-banking vendors keep "user"/staff as a structurally distinct concept from "client"/customer inside their own platforms, frequently delegating staff auth to an entirely separate system (a corporate directory/IdP) rather than the customer-facing login path.
2. **This matches Domain-Driven Design's own test for where a boundary belongs.** Bounded-context boundaries are drawn "where different actors..., different language..., and different rates of change... meet" [search synthesis of DDD sources, Q1/background research]. Customers and staff are different actors with different vocabulary (mandate holder vs. teller/supervisor) and different lifecycle triggers (customer self-registration gated by KYC vs. staff accounts created/removed by an administrator on hiring/termination) — exactly the criteria DDD treats as evidence for a separate context, and GARBATI's own `IdentityApi` design is built, tuned, and audited entirely around the customer actor (decision #12's "no BVN/NIN outside Identity," `CustomerIdentityData`'s stricter access tier, KYC-specific audit events).
3. **Adding staff authentication data to `IdentityApi`'s existing database increases the blast radius of a breach in both directions.** `IdentityApi` today holds regulated customer PII under a specific, carefully reasoned access/retention model (`docs/DESIGN.md` 7.1–7.2). Staff credentials, session state, and role assignments are a different *kind* of sensitive data with a different retention/breach-notification profile; merging them into the same service/database means a compromise of either surface threatens the other, and the carefully separated `CustomerIdentityData` access-control model (7.1) would need to be re-litigated for a second, unrelated kind of sensitive data rather than staying focused on its one job.
4. **GARBATI already has a working precedent for exactly this trade-off**, and it did not choose "graft the new thing onto an existing service's database": `ARCHITECTURE.md` Section 7's deployment guidance for `ProductApi` and `OrchestratorApi` is "design these as separate services, but you do not need separate deployments on day one... Start Product and Orchestrator as separate projects inside one deployable, with the same boundaries enforced in code, and split them later if there is a reason." Applying that same, already-adopted pattern here means: a new staff-identity service, as its own project with its own database/schema (no shared tables, no cross-database FK, matching P-5), can start minimally deployed rather than as a costly separate Azure resource on day one — getting the architectural cleanliness of separation without paying the full operational cost immediately.

### The case for a bounded context inside `IdentityApi` (the honest counter-argument)

1. **No new service, no new deployment, no new pipeline/database/monitoring to pay for or operate** — a real, non-trivial cost at GARBATI's current one-developer stage, and `ARCHITECTURE.md` Section 7 itself names this exact cost explicitly ("each deployed service costs money and needs its own database, pipeline and monitoring").
2. **Reuses infrastructure `IdentityApi` has already built and had QA-reviewed**: its audit-event pattern (Section 6.3), its scope/authorization-policy machinery (Section 5.2), its idempotency conventions, its signed-grant/key-management tooling if staff-session or privileged-action signing is ever needed. A new service starts all of that from zero.
3. **No source found actually requires physical service separation** — the CIAM-vs-IAM distinction (Q1) is a conceptual/product-design separation that every vendor and regulator source discusses in terms of *what the system optimizes for*, not a specific claim about microservice boundaries. A disciplined "new bounded context, new tables, new access-control rules, inside the same deployable" could, in principle, achieve the same logical separation this research found valuable (Q1–Q4) without a second service — provided the owner is confident the team will actually keep that discipline over time, the same caveat `ARCHITECTURE.md` P-6 already raises about modular monoliths generally ("later split not guaranteed easy, depends on keeping... discipline intact in practice").

### Bottom line

Both options can be built responsibly; this research leans toward the **new, small, dedicated service**, built the low-cost way GARBATI already builds new services (separate project, own database, minimal deployment footprint at first) — because the weight of real-world precedent (Q1) and GARBATI's own existing sensitivity model for `IdentityApi`'s customer data (point 3 above) both point toward keeping staff identity as its own clearly-owned thing, not a second kind of sensitive data riding inside a service designed and hardened for a different one. **This is the owner's call, not a resolved question** — the counter-argument (cost, reuse) is real and the owner is better positioned than this research to weigh GARBATI's current budget and timeline against that architectural cleanliness.
