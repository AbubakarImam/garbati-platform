## Plain-language summary (read this first)

GARBATI's `ARCHITECTURE.md` names a "reconciliation job" three times (Section 5/9/12.10) without ever researching what reconciliation actually is. The honest finding of this research: **none of GARBATI's three named gaps is reconciliation in the sense a real bank's back office means it.** Real reconciliation — nostro/vostro matching, core-to-GL reconciliation, NIBSS settlement-file matching — exists because **two separately-maintained records of the same fact**, kept by two different systems or two different institutions, can drift apart, and the match has to tolerate fuzzy timing, approximate amounts, and missing shared identifiers. GARBATI's three gaps are all comparisons **within GARBATI's own already-authoritative data** (a posted ledger leg vs. the rule that should have produced it; an orchestrator's in-flight operation state vs. the ledger's own transactions) — both sides are already owned by GARBATI, already exact-matched by a precise key (idempotency key, product version, chain/leg/purpose), and there is no external counterparty's clock or file format to reconcile against, because GARBATI has no live interbank leg yet. That does not mean the three gaps are not real — it means they are closer to **scheduled consistency-check jobs** than to a reconciliation desk with a break queue, tolerance windows, and a suspense account. Real nostro/vostro-style reconciliation, with everything that comes with it (suspense accounts, aged-item SLAs, a genuine external settlement file to ingest), becomes necessary the day GARBATI's ledger starts actually settling against NIBSS — not before, and not for the three gaps already named.

On NIBSS specifically: this research confirms NIP's settlement mechanics concretely (deferred net settlement, four fixed windows a day, settlement in central-bank money, a 72-hour internal dispute SLA) from a primary-adjacent, directly-read source — but, consistent with T-037/T-041/T-043's experience, several primary NIBSS/CBN documents were unreachable (403s, or PDFs this toolchain could not render), and the exact file format an institution receives from NIBSS at end of day could not be confirmed from any source read directly.

## Q1: How real banks structure reconciliation generally

**Confidence: medium-high** — consistent, convergent description across several independent, named sources (reconciliation-software vendors, a real university's own published GL-reconciliation policy, and one ledger-platform's own technical docs), though none is a regulatory primary source (this question doesn't have one — reconciliation practice is operational convention, not law).

- **End-of-day vs. intraday are genuinely different processes with different purposes, not the same thing run more often.** End-of-day reconciliation checks which payments actually executed and confirmed, and is what drives formal accounting/ERP reconciliation; intraday reconciliation is a treasury-specific, higher-level process used to know how much cash is available to invest or how much must be borrowed *before* the day ends. [AccessPay — What is the importance of reconciliation in finance?](https://accesspay.com/knowledge-hub/connectivity/what-is-the-importance-of-reconciliation-in-finance/)
- **A "break" is the standard term for an unmatched or mismatched item**, arising from value differences outside a pre-set tolerance, missing lines, timing differences in payment processing, unsettled cash legs, fee/interest discrepancies, or outright errors. [AccessPay](https://accesspay.com/knowledge-hub/connectivity/what-is-the-importance-of-reconciliation-in-finance/); [BTRS — Where Breaks Actually Come From in Trade Reconciliation](https://btrsw.com/blog/where-breaks-actually-come-from-trade-reconciliation)
- **Matching keys and tolerance windows are a real, named design choice, not an afterthought.** Exact matching is preferred wherever a shared unique identifier exists; where it doesn't, matching rules expand to amount + date window + counterparty + reference, with an explicit tolerance that reflects genuine, expected differences (bank fees, rounding) rather than treating any such difference as a true break. [AccessPay](https://accesspay.com/knowledge-hub/connectivity/what-is-the-importance-of-reconciliation-in-finance/)
- **Modern Treasury's own ledger platform (a real, named vendor, docs read directly) defines account reconciliation as comparing a ledger-side balance against a counterparty-side balance at a synchronized point in time** (the bank's own timezone and reporting schedule), using a defined priority order of which external balance report to trust (real-time opening ledger first, falling back to the prior day's closing balance) against the ledger's own `posted_balance`. This is a useful concrete example of what "matching" looks like when one side is an internal ledger and the other is an external bank statement — exactly GARBATI's eventual NIBSS situation, not its current one. [Modern Treasury — Balances Used in Account Reconciliation](https://docs.moderntreasury.com/ledgers/docs/balances-used-in-account-reconciliation)
- **Core-to-GL reconciliation inside a single institution (a closer analogue to GARBATI's own internal gaps) is itself a known, named, routine process**: the core banking system tracks customer-account-level balances, the general ledger tracks accounting balances, and the two drift apart because of mapping rules, posting feeds, timing, manual entries, and suspense accounts — not because they are different institutions, but because they are different subsystems maintained on different update rhythms. The standard process described is "pull balances from the core and the GL at the same cutoff, match them by GL account, then drill into product/cost center," with banks typically reconciling core-to-GL daily or monthly, and high-volume/high-risk interfaces checked daily or intraday. [Preql — Core to GL reconciliation for banks](https://www.preql.ai/articles/core-to-gl-reconciliation-for-banks)
- **A real institution's own published reconciliation policy** (not a vendor's marketing description) confirms reconciliation is treated as "a critical and fundamental internal control over financial reporting," required on a fixed cadence (quarterly, in this case) with a fixed submission deadline (45 days after quarter end) and departmental ownership — i.e. reconciliation is organizationally a **back-office/controller function with a compliance cadence**, not something that silently runs itself. [University of Washington — General Ledger Account Reconciliation Policy (GLARP)](https://finance.uw.edu/uco/node/29)
- **Auto-match vs. manual exception handling**: every source describes the same shape — exact/rule-based matching handles the majority automatically, and only the residual (breaks above a threshold) goes to a human for investigation. [Preql](https://www.preql.ai/articles/core-to-gl-reconciliation-for-banks); [AccessPay](https://accesspay.com/knowledge-hub/connectivity/what-is-the-importance-of-reconciliation-in-finance/)
- **BIAN's own service-landscape model** (a bank-architecture standards body, not a vendor) names "Account Reconciliation" as its own service domain — "a general service of account reconciliation between two or more target accounts," aggregated under Accounting Services, explicitly covering journal/ledger-type accounts with nostro/vostro management as a common implementation — confirming reconciliation is treated industry-wide as its own bounded capability, organizationally distinct from account management itself. [BIAN — Account Reconciliation](https://bian.org/servicelandscape-9-1/object_6.html?object=38491)

**What I could not verify:** no single source gives one canonical definition that covers every reconciliation context (nostro, core-to-GL, trade, payments) with the same vocabulary — the synthesis above (break/matching-key/tolerance-window/auto-match-then-exception) is this researcher's convergence across several independently-documented, consistent descriptions, not a single authoritative standard's own wording.

## Q2: Nostro/vostro and interbank-specific reconciliation

**Confidence: medium-high** on the general mechanics (several independent, consistent sources); **medium** on numeric specifics (suspense-account SLA, clearing timelines), which come from fewer, less-authoritative sources.

- **Nostro/vostro reconciliation exists specifically because the same pool of money is recorded independently by two different institutions.** A nostro account is a bank's own account held at a correspondent bank, in a foreign currency or for access to a foreign payment system; the correspondent records the mirror-image vostro account. Reconciliation compares the bank's own expectation of what should have moved against what the correspondent's statement says actually moved — received typically via SWIFT MT940/MT950 messages. [Wikipedia — Nostro and vostro accounts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nostro_and_vostro_accounts); [osfin.ai — Understanding Nostro and Vostro Accounts](https://www.osfin.ai/blog/understanding-nostro-and-vostro-reconciliation-importance-and-process)
- **Discrepancies here are described as constant and largely timing-driven, not exceptional**: a payment debited from the nostro account may not yet be credited to the beneficiary; fees may differ from what was expected; many mismatches "resolve themselves the following day" purely from timing. Best practice pushes toward intraday nostro-confirmation processing specifically to catch this earlier, with full reconciliation expected no later than the day following settlement date. [Sygnity — Reconciliation of Accounts Position](https://www.sygnity.pl/en/banking-finance-and-insurance/reconciliation-of-accounts-position/)
- **A suspense account is a temporary holding account used when a bank cannot immediately identify the correct destination account, customer, or GL code for a transaction** — described as needing clearance within a strict SLA (commonly cited as 30 days) specifically because aged, uncleared suspense items can hide errors, fraud, or unbooked losses. [Panaversity — Bank Reconciliation: Nostro, Suspense, and GL-to-Risk](https://agentfactory.panaversity.org/docs/Business-Domain-Agent-Workflows/banking-domain-agents/reconciliation-nostro-suspense)
- **The New York Fed's own FX-operational-risk guidance** (a genuine regulatory-adjacent primary source, read in search-summary form, not independently re-verified by direct fetch in this research) treats nostro reconciliation as a named operational-risk control specifically for cross-institution FX/correspondent exposure — reinforcing that this category of reconciliation is explicitly about *inter*-institutional risk, not intra-institutional bookkeeping. [New York Fed — Management of Operational Risks in Foreign Exchange](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/microsites/fxc/files/mopsrisk.pdf)

**What I could not verify:** the exact, universal suspense-account clearance SLA (30 days is cited by one secondary, non-regulatory source, not a named regulator's own rule) — this is described everywhere as an institution's own internal policy choice, similar to how `research/reversal-authority-patterns.md` found no universal amount threshold for reversal-approval tiers. I also did not find a primary SWIFT specification for MT940/MT950 directly — their existence and role is corroborated by two independent sources but not read from SWIFT's own documentation.

## Q3: What real NIBSS integration looks like, concretely

**Confidence: medium-high** on the mechanics that were confirmed directly from a primary-adjacent source (the SIIPS 2025 NIP Nigeria case study, co-published by the AfricaNenda Foundation, the UN Economic Commission for Africa, and the World Bank Group — read in full as a PDF, not a search summary); **low**, with real, named access failures, on the exact file/report format a participant institution receives from NIBSS at end of day and on NIBSS's own non-public technical specification.

**Settlement mechanics (confirmed, numeric, directly read):**
- NIP is a **deferred net settlement (DNS)** system: the beneficiary is credited immediately, in real time, while settlement *between* institutions happens later, in batches. "Funds are available online real-time in the beneficiary account before settlement."
- Settlement runs through the **CBN's own Real-Time Gross Settlement (RTGS) system**, with every participating institution holding a dedicated settlement account at the CBN — settlement happens in central-bank money, which eliminates counterparty risk between participants and gives settlement finality.
- **Settlement happens exactly four times a day, at fixed clock times: 10:00, 15:00, 15:30, and 18:00 local time** (cited to CBN, 2020). This has grown over time — NIP settled once a day at launch, moved to twice daily in 2016, then four times daily in 2020, explicitly to improve participant liquidity management and reduce settlement risk.
- To participate, institutions must post **collateral — Federal Government Treasury Bills at 110% of their own largest historic net debit position** — a concrete, named credit-risk mitigant for the deferred-net part of DNS.
- **Transaction flow, concretely, who initiates vs. confirms (9 numbered steps in the source):** (1) sender initiates payment at their own institution; (2) the sender's own institution validates the transaction; (3) the transaction message is transmitted to the NIBSS NIP switch; (4) NIBSS verifies and routes it to the recipient's institution; (5) the recipient's institution credits the recipient's account/wallet; (6) the recipient receives the payment instantly; (7) the recipient's institution sends an acknowledgment message back to NIBSS; (8) NIBSS logs the transaction and sends a completion confirmation to the sender's institution; (9) both sender and recipient get final notifications. **NIBSS itself never holds the money mid-flight — it is a message router and, separately, the settlement-calculation layer; the recipient's own institution decides to credit before interbank settlement has actually happened.**
- **Maximum transaction processing time is 20 seconds** (down from a 45-second maximum reported in an earlier edition of the same case-study series), though most transactions complete in under one second.
[AfricaNenda / UN ECA / World Bank Group — SIIPS 2025 Case Study: NIP Nigeria](https://www.africanenda.org/uploads/files/siips2025/siips_2025_NIP-Nigeria_CaseStudy_en.pdf) (fetched and read in full as a PDF for this research)

**Message format (confirmed, but with a live discrepancy worth flagging):**
- NIP's own messaging standard today is described as **"XML-based messaging format developed in-house"** — not ISO 20022, not ISO 8583. The same source states NIBSS is "actively transitioning to the ISO 20022 messaging standard," with "initial transactional phases targeted for completion within the year [2025]." ISO 20022 is confirmed already live for one specific use case: NIBSS's cross-border aggregator role for PAPSS (the Pan-African Payment and Settlement System) converts PAPSS's ISO 20022 messages into NIP's local format, not the other way round — i.e. as of this source, domestic NIP traffic itself was still not natively ISO 20022.
- **This is worth flagging as a real, unresolved discrepancy, not papered over:** this SIIPS source (published November 2025, reflecting research likely conducted mid-2025) frames ISO 20022 completion as a 2025 target; `research/oss-core-banking-readiness-gaps.md`'s G12 (dated October 2026, citing ThisDay) describes the National Payment Stack as being in "active rollout" as of October 2026. Read together, the most honest reading is that the domestic ISO 20022 migration is taking longer, or is being rolled out in more phases, than NIBSS's own stated 2025 target — not that the two sources contradict each other on the underlying fact that the migration is real and in progress. Neither this brief nor G12 can confirm the current, actual completion state as of October 2026 from a source read directly.
- For context, two other NIBSS-published technical specifications (found by title, not read in full) confirm NIBSS uses **ISO 8583** for its POS interface specifically (a different channel from NIP) — [search result: "NIBSS POS Interface Specification Ver 1.16," based on ISO 8583 (1987)] — meaning NIBSS's messaging landscape is genuinely mixed across channels (in-house XML for NIP, ISO 8583 for POS, migrating toward ISO 20022), not a single uniform standard even today.

**Participant structure — directly relevant to GARBATI's own target customer (microfinance institutions):**
- NIP has **29 direct participants (all commercial banks)** and **670 indirect participants**, broken down as **541 microfinance banks, 10 mobile money operators, 5 payment service banks, 20 primary mortgage banks, 2 development finance institutions, and 92 other financial institutions.** Participation is mandatory for all deposit-taking institutions under the 2018 CBN Regulation on Instant (Inter-Bank) Electronic Funds Transfer Services in Nigeria.
- **Indirect participants connect to NIBSS through a sponsor/settlement relationship with a direct participant bank, not directly to NIBSS.** This is confirmed by a second, independent, directly-read source: a commercial bank's own published settlement-bank-services page states it performs "NET settlement into nominated accounts on behalf of indirect participants," offers "automated statement generation (intraday, end-of-day, weekly, monthly)" and "automatic account reconciliation on company ERP via integrated reporting," plus daily settlement reports and chargeback information, specifically for clients it onboards onto NIP as a sponsor. [Stanbic IBTC Bank — Settlement Bank Services](https://www.stanbicibtcbank.com/nigeriabank/corporate-and-investment/transaction-banking/Settlement-Bank-Services)
- **This is a concrete, GARBATI-specific finding worth naming plainly:** since GARBATI is built for microfinance institutions, and MFIs are the single largest indirect-participant category on NIP (541 of 670), a real future GARBATI/NIBSS integration most likely would not be "GARBATI's LedgerApi talks to NIBSS directly." It would be "the adopting institution's settlement account sits with a sponsor bank, and the adopting institution reconciles against *that sponsor bank's* settlement reports" — which in turn reconciles against NIBSS. That is potentially **two reconciliation hops, not one**, and the second hop (sponsor bank to NIBSS) is not GARBATI's problem to solve at all. This reframes what "NIBSS integration" would even mean for GARBATI specifically, and nothing in `ARCHITECTURE.md`'s existing three reconciliation mentions anticipates this two-hop shape.

**Dispute/timing SLA (confirmed, numeric):**
- NIBSS runs its own internal **Dispute Resolution System (DRS)** for NIP specifically: "DRS requires banks to resolve transaction disputes within 72 hours, with automatic triggers for systemic defaults if the participant does not meet this timeline," with escalation to the CBN's own consumer protection department (established 2012) as the next layer. This is a separate, independently-sourced figure from, but broadly consistent in shape with, `research/reversal-authority-patterns.md`'s own CBN findings (Q5 there) — that brief found a roughly "2 working days" instant-EFT-dispute-resolution figure and a "no more than 2 weeks" lien/mediation window from a different source (Mondaq's reading of the 2018 CBN Regulation). The two sets of numbers are not identical (72 hours is roughly 3 days, not 2) and should not be treated as the same figure restated — they may describe different stages of the same dispute lifecycle (an initial 72-hour internal DRS step vs. a longer CBN-level escalation/lien window), but this research did not find a single source reconciling them into one timeline.

**What was blocked or could not be confirmed — stated plainly, consistent with T-037/T-041/T-043's documented pattern of Nigerian regulatory/financial-infrastructure sources being unreachable:**
- **NIBSS's own site** (`nibss-plc.com.ng`) gave only marketing-level content on settlement/reconciliation when fetched directly — no settlement windows, no message-format detail, no reconciliation-report description — and explicitly directs anyone needing operational detail to "contact your Relationship Manager at NIBSS." A referenced "NIP Technical Specification Document Ver 7.0" (named in a secondary industry article) could not be located publicly.
- **The CBN's own "exposure circular for indirect participants" PDF** (`cbn.gov.ng/out/2018/psmd/...`) returned **HTTP 403 Forbidden** on direct fetch — the same access failure pattern `research/nigerian-regulatory-requirements.md`, T-041 and T-043 each already documented repeatedly for cbn.gov.ng primary documents. This specific document, by its own title, is exactly the kind of primary source that would describe indirect-participant settlement/reconciliation obligations in detail — it could not be read.
- **NIBSS's own "Guidelines on Operations of Electronic Payment Channels in Nigeria" PDF** was fetched successfully as raw bytes, but this toolchain could not render it as readable text (a missing local PDF-rendering dependency, `poppler-utils`/`pdftoppm`, not a network block) — a different kind of blocked access than a 403, but the practical result is the same: this document's content on settlement-report submission deadlines and reconciliation requirements could not be verified by this research. A search-engine summary (not independently verified) suggested settlement reports must reach NIBSS "daily by 10am for the previous day," but this figure rests on an unread secondary snippet, not a source this research actually read, and should not be relied on.
- **The underlying World Bank 2021 NIBSS case study PDF** (an earlier document in the same lineage as the SIIPS 2025 case study this research did read) returned 403 on direct fetch; the SIIPS 2025 case study (co-published by the World Bank Group) is the newer, successfully-read document in the same family and is treated as the authoritative source above.
- **The exact file/report format a participant institution receives from NIBSS at end of day** (a settlement file analogous to a nostro statement) was not confirmed from any source read directly in this research — every source either didn't address it or pointed to non-public, relationship-manager-gated documentation. This is a genuine, named gap, not a guess.

## Q4: How this maps onto GARBATI's three already-named reconciliation gaps

Taking each of `ARCHITECTURE.md`'s three mentions in turn, against what Q1-Q3 actually found:

**Gap 1 — "a reconciliation job should compare posted fees with ProductApi's rules"** (Section 9, under "What the token cannot cover"). This is **not** nostro/vostro-style reconciliation at all — it is a one-institution, one-system check between a `Fee`/`Tax` leg LedgerApi already posted and the `FeeRule` that should have produced it, and GARBATI already owns both sides. It is closer to the "core-to-GL" pattern from Q1 (Preql) than to Q2's nostro pattern — except even simpler, because `ARCHITECTURE.md` decision P-11 (immutable, versioned product rules; "every fee records the version used") already removes the single biggest source of real-world core-to-GL drift (rules changing between when a figure was computed and when it's checked). Given that, this check can be a **deterministic recomputation and comparison** — for each posted `Fee`/`Tax` leg, re-run `ProductApi.FeeRule(recorded version).compute(principal)` and compare to the leg's actual amount — not a fuzzy-matching reconciliation with tolerance windows and a break queue. The real-world pattern (Q1) is the right *shape* (periodic batch check, auto-pass vs. exception), but GARBATI's own existing architecture (versioned rules) already does work a real institution's reconciliation function has to do the hard way.

**Gap 2 — "orchestrator crash mid-operation... a reconciliation job that checks the ledger by idempotency key"** (Section 9). This is the gap closest in *spirit* to real reconciliation's core use case (Q1/Q2): two independently-updated records of the same operation — OrchestratorApi's own in-flight operation state, and LedgerApi's actually-posted transactions — that can genuinely diverge because of a crash between steps, the same way a nostro debit can be recorded before the matching credit lands. But it is still intra-platform: both sides are owned by GARBATI, and the matching key (the client-supplied idempotency key, decision #42/#44) is **exact and unique by construction**, not the fuzzy amount/date/counterparty matching real nostro reconciliation needs because two separate organizations don't share a perfect identifier. This makes Gap 2 closer to a **scheduled sweep/garbage-collection job** (find reservations past an expiry with no matching posted idempotency key; retry-forward or release) than to a reconciliation desk function.

**Gap 3 — the grant-token's "fee and tax legs are not covered by the token... the reconciliation job in Section 9 checks them"** (Section 12.10). Reading this against Gap 1: this is **the same check as Gap 1**, described from the authorization-integrity angle rather than the fee-accuracy angle — Section 12.10 literally points back to Section 9's job rather than describing a separate one. So GARBATI's "three" named gaps are honestly **two distinct checks, not three**: (a) does a posted Fee/Tax leg match what ProductApi's versioned rule says it should be (Gaps 1 and 3, same job), and (b) did an orchestrator crash leave an orphaned reservation with no matching ledger post (Gap 2).

**The honest, overall finding for Q4:** none of GARBATI's three named gaps needs nostro/vostro-style reconciliation machinery — no suspense account, no fuzzy matching, no external settlement file, no break-aging SLA. That machinery becomes necessary specifically because of what Q2/Q3 found: an *external* counterparty with its own clock, its own file format, and imperfect shared identifiers. GARBATI doesn't have one of those yet. What GARBATI's own architecture (decision #1's append-only ledger, decision #45's atomic chains with exact purpose codes and idempotency keys, P-11's versioned product rules) already provides is precisely the thing real reconciliation has to work hard to approximate: a single, authoritative, exactly-keyed record on both sides of each comparison. That is a genuine structural advantage worth naming plainly, not assumed away by importing a textbook reconciliation-service design wholesale.

## Q5: Dedicated service, or inside LedgerApi — a structural question, not a decision

This section states the real argument on each side, grounded in Q1-Q4 and in `ARCHITECTURE.md`'s own existing rules — it recommends nothing and decides nothing, per the task's explicit scope.

**The strongest argument against putting this inside `LedgerApi` itself is structural, not a generic microservices opinion:** `ARCHITECTURE.md`'s decision #34 (reaffirmed in Section 11 item 5) states plainly that **"LedgerApi calls nobody"** — it checks a decision token locally and posts what it's given; it has no outbound dependency on `IdentityApi`, `ProductApi`, or `OrchestratorApi` at all (Section 3's dependency diagram shows no arrow leaving `LedgerApi`). But both of the real checks found in Q4 are inherently cross-service: Gap 1/3 (fee-vs-rule) needs to read a posted `Fee`/`Tax` leg *from LedgerApi* and the matching `FeeRule` version *from ProductApi*, and compare them — exactly the kind of "anything needing both goes through the orchestrator" situation Section 3 already names for `IdentityApi`/`ProductApi`. Gap 2 (crash sweep) needs to read in-flight operation state *from OrchestratorApi* and posted transactions *from LedgerApi*. **Neither check can live purely inside `LedgerApi` without either breaking decision #34 (LedgerApi starts calling other services) or duplicating another service's data inside LedgerApi's own database (breaking P-5, no shared DB / no cross-service copies).** This is a real, GARBATI-specific argument, not an abstract one.

**The argument for this looking more like a feature than a new standalone service, at least for the two gaps GARBATI has today:** Q4 found both checks are lightweight, exact-keyed, and largely deterministic — not a reconciliation desk with a human exception queue, tolerance windows, and aging SLAs. Something that shape fits naturally as a scheduled background job *inside an existing orchestrating service* — most plausibly `OrchestratorApi`, which already plays the "orchestrates, owns no truth, calls the others" role the two checks need, and which already owns "in-flight operation state" per the service map (Section 2) — read-only, calling `LedgerApi`'s and `ProductApi`'s existing read endpoints the same way `OrchestratorApi` already does for every live operation, rather than a new service with its own database, deployment, and API surface.

**The argument for a dedicated future service (the `ScheduleApi`/financing/`Channel` "later tranche" pattern) is forward-looking, not about today's two checks:** the moment GARBATI has a real NIBSS leg, Q2/Q3's findings apply in full — a genuine external settlement file to ingest on NIBSS's own four-times-daily schedule, fuzzy matching against an outside party's record, a suspense account to hold unmatched interbank credits, an aging SLA to clear it down, and (per Q3's two-hop finding) potentially a sponsor-bank relationship in between. That is categorically more than a background consistency-check job, and it is the kind of thing that plausibly deserves its own owner, database, and operational runbook — the same reasoning that justified `ScheduleApi` as separate from the orchestrator (its own run history and retry logic, P-3) rather than a feature bolted onto something else.

**The honest, non-decided synthesis:** GARBATI's three already-named gaps, as they actually exist today, look like they fit as a feature/job inside an existing service (most plausibly `OrchestratorApi`, for the structural reasons above) rather than justifying a new dedicated `ReconciliationService` on their own. A dedicated `ReconciliationService` becomes a much stronger case the day real NIBSS settlement exists — but that is explicitly not assumed imminent here, and this research does not resolve which shape the eventual owner should choose, only what the real tradeoff is and why LedgerApi itself is structurally the wrong place for either version.
