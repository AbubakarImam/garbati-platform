# T-021: Research — lien / auto-sweep patterns for unrecoverable reversals

Output file: `research/lien-auto-sweep-patterns.md`. This is research only — no code, no design decision. It refines decision #34 in `Garbati.IdentityApi/docs/DESIGN.md` Section 8.6 (`ReversalOutcomes`, the write-off record), specifically testing the owner's belief that real systems place a standing lien for the unrecoverable amount and automatically debit it later the moment money lands in the account, rather than treating "recoverable" vs "write-off" as a one-time manual decision.

## Context

`research/reversal-authority-patterns.md` (T-019) already found, from Nigeria's CBN instant-EFT framework: "a lien placed on disputed funds pending resolution lasts no more than 2 weeks." But that research did not establish **what the lien actually does mechanically** — does it only hold funds already in the account at the time of the dispute, or does it also capture future incoming funds automatically, debiting them the moment they arrive, without a new manual decision each time? Read `research/reversal-authority-patterns.md` Q4 and Q5 in full before starting — do not duplicate that research, extend it on this one specific mechanical question.

`Garbati.IdentityApi/docs/DESIGN.md` Section 8.6 currently treats a reversal's outcome as binary and decided once: `Completed` or `WriteOff`, recorded by a human/ops action at some point. The owner's belief, to be checked against real systems: when money is genuinely unrecoverable *right now* because the account lacks funds, real systems don't necessarily write it off immediately — they place a **standing lien/hold for the owed amount** and **automatically** sweep/debit it the instant sufficient funds later arrive in that account, with no new manual approval needed at that moment. This is a materially different mechanism than "decide once, write off once" — it implies an account can carry an outstanding, automatically-enforced debt that silently resolves itself whenever money shows up, for as long as the lien stays active.

## Questions to answer

1. **Does this standing-lien-with-auto-sweep mechanism exist in real systems, and under what name?** Search specifically for mechanisms like "administrative hold," "negative balance recovery," "debit block," "right of offset" / "right of setoff," "auto-sweep," "standing lien," or similar — in core banking systems, card issuer overdraft-recovery practice, or payment-rail erroneous-credit recovery (NACHA, CBN, UK Faster Payments misdirected-payment practice already found in T-019). Is this the *standard* way real institutions handle "the money's gone but the account might get funded again later," or is a one-time write-off decision (T-019's finding) actually the more common pattern, with auto-sweep being a specific sub-case?
2. **Is the actual debit automatic, or does a human still approve it at the moment funds arrive?** If a lien exists, does the bank's system auto-execute the debit the instant the balance check passes (no new authorization step), or does arriving funds just trigger a *notification/queue* for a human to then approve the actual debit? This is the crux of the owner's question — distinguish "fully automatic" from "automatically detected, still manually approved."
3. **How long does such a lien/hold typically remain active, and what happens when it expires?** Does it convert to the write-off/collections process T-019 already found once expired, or can it be renewed/extended? Is there a regulatory limit on how long a bank may hold such a lien (cross-check against CBN's "no more than 2 weeks" finding from T-019 — does that 2-week figure describe this exact mechanism, or a different kind of lien, e.g. one on funds already present rather than a standing claim on future funds)?
4. **What is the legal/regulatory basis for debiting an account later without fresh customer consent, specifically for recovering an erroneous/unauthorized credit?** This is distinct from a bank's general right of setoff for a customer's own debt (e.g. an unpaid loan) — focus specifically on recovering money that arrived in error, not a pre-existing contractual debt. Does this require the original account-opening terms to pre-authorize it, or does banking law/regulation grant this right independent of contract terms?
5. **Does Nigeria's CBN framework specifically include an auto-sweep mechanism**, or does its 2-week lien only block/freeze funds already present, with no mechanic for automatically capturing *future* incoming funds? Re-read the CBN sources T-019 already found (the Mondaq article and the 2018/2020 circulars) specifically for this mechanical detail, and search for anything additional if those sources don't settle it.
