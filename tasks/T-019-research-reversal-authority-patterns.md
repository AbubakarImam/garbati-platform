# T-019: Research — reversal/dispute authorization patterns in banking

Output file: `research/reversal-authority-patterns.md`. This is research only — no code, no repo other than `garbati-platform`. It exists to inform T-015 (designing IdentityApi's reversal authority), which the owner deliberately deferred rather than decide quickly, since `research/identityapi-design-review.md` flagged it as the single item most clearly tied to real financial-loss risk if left vague.

## Context

`Garbati_IdentityApi_Design.md`'s original v1 draft (preserved in `Garbati.IdentityApi`'s git history) left this explicitly open in its Section 9: "Define who may initiate a reversal, required reason codes/approvals, the expected operational response time, and how reconciliation handles external effects that a ledger reversal cannot undo by itself." `Garbati.IdentityApi/docs/DESIGN.md`'s current Section 8 repeats this as explicitly **not designed**.

What's already decided, at the **ledger mechanics** level (do not re-litigate these, they're fixed): a chain reversal undoes every leg, last leg first, all-or-nothing (LedgerApi decision #45); if any leg can't be reversed (e.g. the recipient already spent the money), the **whole** reversal fails and nothing is refunded, with no remediation path defined yet (`ARCHITECTURE.md` Section 9, D-7). What's missing is everything **above** the ledger: who is allowed to ask for a reversal in the first place, what has to be true before IdentityApi lets that request through, and what happens operationally when the ledger says "can't do it."

## Questions to answer

1. **Who initiates a reversal, in practice?** In real banking/payments systems, is a reversal typically customer-self-service (like disputing a charge), initiated only by an internal ops/support agent on the customer's behalf, fully automated for certain cases (e.g. a bounced/failed settlement), or some mix depending on the reason? Is there a standard split between "the bank reverses its own error" vs. "a customer disputes someone else's action"?
2. **What approval is required, and does it scale with risk?** Do real systems require extra approval (e.g. a supervisor, a second reviewer — the same "maker-checker" idea already used elsewhere in this design) for reversals above a certain amount or of a certain type? For a **joint account**, does reversing a transaction need the same multi-holder consent the original transaction needed, or is reversal treated differently (e.g. any one holder, or only the original requester, or an ops override)?
3. **Standard reason codes.** Do established standards already define a reason-code set GARBATI could reuse instead of inventing one — ISO 20022 payment return/reversal reason codes, card network (Visa/Mastercard) chargeback reason categories, or similar? What do the major categories look like (fraud, duplicate, customer dispute, bank error, insufficient funds, etc.)?
4. **Reconciliation when a reversal can't fully undo an external effect.** When the money is simply gone (recipient already withdrew/spent it) and the all-or-nothing ledger reversal therefore fails, what do real institutions do operationally — write-off/loss accounting, a collections process against the recipient, insurance/reserve funds, regulatory reporting requirements for unrecovered amounts? Is there a standard distinction between "we can claw this back" and "this is now a loss to absorb"?
5. **Operational response-time expectations.** Do standards or common practice distinguish response-time expectations by reversal type (e.g. "bank's own error, reverse immediately" vs. "customer dispute, investigate within N business days")? Any relevant regulatory timelines (e.g. card network chargeback windows, or — if findable — anything Nigeria/CBN-specific, cross-checking `research/nigerian-regulatory-requirements.md`'s existing findings rather than duplicating that research)?

## Format

Write `research/reversal-authority-patterns.md` with: a plain-language summary up top (define jargon — "chargeback," "write-off," "maker-checker" if reused, on first use), sourced answers to the five questions, then a **"Recommended default shape for GARBATI"** section — a sourced, concrete recommendation (not a decision) covering who may initiate, what approval scales with what risk, a starter reason-code set, and a reconciliation/write-off process shape — explicitly labeled as a recommendation for the owner to accept, change, or reject. End with a "What this does not settle" section: this does not decide anything, is not a Sharia or Nigerian-regulatory ruling (flag anything Sharia-relevant you find, e.g. around write-offs/loss-absorption, but do not treat it as settled), and does not replace the owner's own judgment.
