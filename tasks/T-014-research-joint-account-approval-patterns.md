It exists to inform a real decision the owner is about to make for IdentityApi's design (feeding D-9): joint accounts with a **configurable** approval threshold (an account can be set up so any one holder may act alone, or so N of its M holders must approve before a debit is authorized).

## Context

Read `/Users/USER/Downloads/Garbati_IdentityApi_Design.md` in full first — specifically Section 2.2 (Mandate model) and Section 3 (mandate rules and transaction authorization), which currently assume **one** active mandate per account issuing a grant after a single check. This is a materially different flow from what the draft currently describes: instead of "check one mandate, issue a grant," it becomes "collect approvals from enough holders before a grant can be issued" — a new, stateful, multi-party flow with its own timeout question, distinct from the existing single-grant 30-second expiry (P-12).

## Questions to answer

1. **Named pattern(s).** What is this kind of control called in the literature — "dual control," "maker-checker" (common in banking/treasury operations), "M-of-N" or "k-of-N" threshold approval (common in multisig cryptocurrency wallets and corporate payment-approval systems)? Are these the same underlying pattern applied in different domains, or do they differ in a way that matters for GARBATI? Cite how each domain (banking treasury operations, multisig wallets, enterprise payment-approval software) describes its own version.
2. **Threshold semantics.** Is "any N of M holders, unordered" (a simple count threshold) the standard shape, or do real systems commonly support naming specific required approvers (e.g., "must include the primary/eldest holder," or weighted votes)? What do standard sources recommend as the simplest safe default, and what do they warn about more complex schemes?
3. **Pending-approval timeout.** For a multi-party approval that is waiting on a human to act (not an automated single-service check), what do real systems use as a typical window before the pending request expires (minutes? hours? days?) — contrast this with GARBATI's existing single-grant 30-second window, which assumes no human is in the loop. What happens, in standard designs, to a transaction whose approval request times out — does it just vanish, or does it need an explicit "expired, start over" state recorded?
4. **Reconfiguration safeguards.** Who is typically allowed to change an account's required-approval threshold, and does changing it (e.g., lowering "requires 2 of 2" down to "any 1 of 2") itself require approval from the existing holders — specifically to prevent one joint holder from unilaterally weakening the account's protection without the others' consent? Cite how real multisig/dual-control systems handle changing their own threshold.
5. **Partial-approval failure modes.** If one holder approves a pending request and is then suspended, revoked, or dies before the threshold is met, what do standard sources say should happen to that holder's already-given partial approval — does it stay valid, or must approval restart? Also: can the same holder's approval be reused across multiple pending requests, or must each request collect fresh approvals every time (tie this to the existing single-use/replay rules already established for the single-holder grant)?
